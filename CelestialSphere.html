Ecliptic and planetary motion

The planets circle the Sun close to one flat plane, the ecliptic plane. Each orbit tips a little away from it.

Orbit sizes are not to scale, and the tilts are drawn larger than life so you can see them. Mercury tilts most, at 7°. Earth's orbit defines the ecliptic, so its tilt is 0°.

Zodiac belt (Bhachakra)

The ecliptic runs along the middle of a band that reaches 9° north and 9° south of it. The band is split into twelve signs of 30° each, counted from the start of Mesha.

The highlighted sign is the one the Sun appears in. Seen from Earth, the Sun lies on the opposite side of the orbit from Earth, so the dashed line runs from Earth through the Sun to the belt. Degrees are sidereal, using the same 24.2° shift as the sphere above.

Reference

The three lecture slides these sections are based on, with a short glossary of the terms they use.

Celestial sphere
An imaginary sphere around Earth. The stars, Sun, Moon and planets appear to sit on it.
Celestial equator
Earth's equator projected out onto the sphere.
Ecliptic
The Sun's apparent path across the sky over a year. The ecliptic plane is the flat plane of Earth's orbit.
Earth's tilt
The angle between the celestial equator and the ecliptic, about 23.4° (the slide writes 23.5°). It is also the angle between Earth's axis and the ecliptic pole.
Equinoxes
The two points where the ecliptic crosses the celestial equator. The Sun reaches the vernal equinox around 20 March, moving north, and the autumnal equinox around 23 September.
Solstices
The two points on the ecliptic farthest from the celestial equator, reached around 21 June and 21 December.
Zodiac belt (Bhachakra)
The band of sky about 9° either side of the ecliptic, where the Sun, Moon and planets always appear. It is divided into twelve signs of 30° each, starting from Mesha (Aries) at 0°.
Rahu and Ketu
The two points where the Moon's orbit crosses the ecliptic. Rahu is the ascending node and Ketu the descending node, opposite each other. The Moon's orbit is tilted about 5.1° to the ecliptic, and the nodes move backwards through the signs, one lap in about 18.6 years.
Orbital tilts
Mercury 7°, Venus 3.4°, Earth 0°, Mars 1.8°, Jupiter 1.3° and Saturn 2.5°, measured from the ecliptic. The slide shows Jupiter at about 1.5°.

Class notes

Ecliptic plane (Apamandala)
The plane in which Earth revolves around the Sun. Seen from Earth, it appears as the Sun revolving around Earth. This is the geocentric plane.
Celestial sphere
The 360° of our solar system.
Equator
The plane perpendicular to Earth's axis, which is tilted 23.5°.
Equinox
Day and night of equal duration.
Solstice (winter, summer)
Day or night is longest. At the summer solstice the day is longest, and at the winter solstice the night is longest (in the northern hemisphere).
