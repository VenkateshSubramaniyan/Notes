Graha Yuti — Two-Planet Conjunction Reference

Classical characteristics of all 35 planetary conjunctions — how rare each pairing is in the sky, and how it reads when strong versus weak — with lagna-wise notes on when they rise into full yogas.

Framework — Lagna, Rarity, and Strength

A conjunction's core nature is constant across every chart; lagna changes which houses the two planets rule; strength (exalted/own sign/kendra-trikona vs. debilitated/combust/dusthana/afflicted) decides whether it shows its best or worst face. Rarity reflects how often the two planets actually return to conjunction in the sky (their synodic cycle) — not the odds of it landing in any one person's chart. Fast-moving grahas (Moon, Mercury, Venus, Sun) catch up to almost anything within months, so most conjunctions involving them recur often. The three slow-movers — Jupiter, Saturn, and the lunar nodes — take years to decades to meet each other again, which is why Jupiter–Saturn ("the Great Conjunction") is genuinely rare. Each Rarity cell also shows Together — roughly how long the pair stays within the same sign once conjunct, set by whichever of the two planets is faster (it moves on first, ending the conjunction). Retrograde loops can stretch these windows well beyond the average shown.

Single Planets (Unconjoined)

Before layering on conjunctions, it helps to know what each planet does when it sits alone in a house — no other graha sharing the sign to blend, soften, or complicate its expression. An unconjoined planet shows its purest, least diluted signification; whether that's a gift or a liability still depends entirely on its own strength (exalted, own sign, debilitated, combust) and the house/lagna it occupies, which is what the Strong/Weak columns below capture.

RA Amplified, unconventional desire and ambition focused entirely on that one house — an area the person obsesses over or chases intensely. Bold, innovative success in that domain through unconventional means; comfortable breaking new ground there. Obsessive craving, confusion, or illusion concentrated in that house; chasing something ultimately unsatisfying.
KE Detachment and past-life familiarity concentrated in that single house — the person often shows unusual skill there but little sustained interest. Intuitive mastery or spiritual insight in that domain, achieved with surprising ease and little ego attachment. Confusion, disinterest, or a sense of loss specifically tied to that house; difficulty committing to that life area.

"Alone" here means no other graha shares the sign — but the house itself, the sign's ruler, and any aspects landing on the planet still shape the reading heavily. An unconjoined planet is simpler to read, not automatically stronger or weaker.

A conjunction in a dusthana (6th/8th/12th) behaves very differently from the same pair in a kendra/trikona, regardless of lagna — and the If Strong / If Weak columns above should always be weighed together with this lagna context. True yogakarakas (one planet ruling both a kendra and a trikona) only arise for six lagnas — Taurus, Cancer, Leo, Libra, Capricorn, and Aquarius — via Mars, Saturn, or Venus as shown above. The remaining six lagnas (the movable and dual signs: Aries, Gemini, Virgo, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Pisces) simply don't produce one, so their strongest conjunctions come from ordinary raja-yoga-forming lord combinations instead.

Jaimini Astrology — The Chara Karakas

Where Parashari astrology assigns each planet a fixed role (Sun = father, Moon = mother, always), Jaimini astrology layers on a second, chart-specific system: the Chara Karakas ("variable significators"). For every birth chart, the seven classical planets (Sun through Saturn, excluding the always-opposite lunar nodes by default) are ranked purely by their degree within their own sign — highest degree first, lowest last — regardless of which sign or house they sit in. Whichever planet lands in each rank position takes on that role's meaning for that specific person. Some traditions extend the ranking to include Rahu, producing an 8-karaka scheme instead of 7; both approaches are used by different lineages.

A few practical notes: the Chara Karakas don't replace a planet's usual Parashari significations, they add a soul-specific layer on top — the Moon is still the general signifier of mind and mother, but whichever planet becomes someone's Matrikaraka shows the particular karmic flavor of that mother relationship. Whether to use the 7-karaka scheme (excluding Rahu) or the 8-karaka scheme (including Rahu) is a genuine, long-standing split between traditional lineages — both are considered valid starting points for analysis. Ketu is generally excluded from the ranking in either scheme.

Functional Roles — Benefic & Malefic Planets by Lagna (Parashari)

A planet's natural nature (Jupiter and Venus are naturally benefic; Mars, Saturn, the Sun, Rahu, and Ketu are naturally malefic) is only the starting point. In Parashari astrology, what actually matters for a given birth chart is its functional nature — decided by which houses it rules from that specific lagna. A planet ruling a trikona (5th/9th) turns favorable almost regardless of its natural character; a planet ruling only a dusthana (6th/8th/12th) turns unfavorable almost regardless of its natural character. A natural benefic that rules only a kendra (4th/7th/10th) with no trikona loses much of its benefic strength; a natural malefic in the same position loses much of its malefic sting. The 2nd and 7th lords carry additional "maraka" (life-force-testing) weight in their dashas, independent of this benefic/malefic classification.

Where a planet rules two houses of conflicting nature (e.g. a trikona and a dusthana together), the general convention is that trikona and lagna lordship dominate the result, so the planet is listed as a benefic — but its results carry a mixed flavor and reward closer individual analysis rather than a blanket reading.

The Natural Significators (Naisargika Karakas)

This is the third layer, and the oldest one — fixed significations every planet carries in every chart, regardless of lagna or degree. How the three layers work together in practice: the Naisargika Karaka tells you what a planet generally means; the functional benefic/malefic table tells you whether that planet is likely to help or hinder in this specific lagna; and the Chara Karaka tells you which planet is playing which soul-role in this specific chart based on degree. All three are read together, not in isolation.

The Twelve Houses (Bhavas)

If planets are the actors, the twelve houses (bhavas) are the stages — each one governs a distinct slice of life experience. Every house has a one-word question it answers about the native; that question is the fastest way to remember what it actually covers before diving into the detailed significations. The Natural Lord column below uses the Kalapurusha (cosmic being) framework — the default zodiac with Aries as the 1st house — which is why it never changes; a real birth chart's actual house lords depend on the lagna and shift accordingly (see the Functional Roles table above for that lagna-specific version).

House groupings worth remembering: Kendras (1, 4, 7, 10) are the pillars of the chart — action and stability; Trikonas (1, 5, 9) are the houses of fortune and dharma — the source of genuine benefic strength; Dusthanas (6, 8, 12) are the difficult houses — struggle, transformation, and loss; Upachayas (3, 6, 10, 11) are the houses that improve with effort and age. Every table earlier in this document — strength, rarity, yogakaraka status — is ultimately built on these four groupings.
