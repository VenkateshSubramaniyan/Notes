Vedic astrology · Timing reference

Time-based events in Vedic astrology

A simple guide to the main timing layers, with a detailed section on Saturn (Sani), whose named Moon-based transit periods are especially prominent in Tamil astrology.

1. The main timing layers

Long- and medium-term

Dasha systems

Mahadasha, Antardasha, Pratyantardasha and finer subdivisions indicate planetary periods in systems such as Vimshottari. Other systems include Yogini, Ashtottari, Chara and Narayana Dasha.

Current planetary positions

Gochara / Peyarchi

Transits describe planets moving through signs and houses relative to the natal chart. Sani Peyarchi means Saturn's sign ingress; Guru Peyarchi means Jupiter's sign ingress, and so on.

Special named transit periods

Named Sani transits

Ezharai Sani, Ashtama Sani and Ardha Ashtama Sani are defined by Saturn's sign position from the natal Moon. Kantaka Sani is used differently across traditions.

Shorter timing triggers

Transit conditions

Retrograde/direct motion, stations, conjunctions, aspects, combustion, eclipses, nakshatra changes and exact crossings can refine a transit reading. They are not all separate named periods.

Cycles and annual methods

Returns and Varshaphala

Saturn, Jupiter and nodal returns mark returns to natal positions. Varshaphala uses an annual chart; it is a separate timing framework.

Specific timing questions

Muhurta and Prashna

Muhurta selects a traditionally favourable time to begin something. Prashna interprets a chart cast for a particular question. Neither is simply another transit period.

2. Sani in detail

These names are generally calculated from Saturn's position relative to the natal Moon sign (Janma Rasi). Duration is approximate; sign-entry dates depend on the sidereal calculation and retrograde motion.

About 7½ years

Ezharai Sani / Sade Sati

Saturn transits the 12th, 1st and 2nd signs from natal Moon

This is a three-phase sequence, with Saturn spending roughly 2½ years in each sign. The three phases are often discussed together as one 7½-year period.

Phase 1 · about 2½ years

Viraya Sani

Saturn in the 12th from natal Moon

The first phase of Ezharai Sani. Traditional themes include expenditure, withdrawal, distant places and changes to established routines. The label does not guarantee loss or separation.

Phase 2 · about 2½ years

Janma Sani

Saturn in the Moon sign

The middle phase. Traditional readings focus on personal responsibilities, identity, stamina and emotional pressure. It means Saturn is in the same sign as the natal Moon, not necessarily exactly conjunct its degree.

Phase 3 · about 2½ years

Final Ezharai phase

Saturn in the 2nd from natal Moon

The final phase. Traditional themes include family obligations, speech, savings and financial discipline. It completes the three-sign sequence.

About 2½ years

Ashtama Sani

Saturn in the 8th from natal Moon

Traditionally associated with uncertainty, transformation, shared resources, taxes, insurance, research and confronting difficult matters. It is not the same as Saturn transiting the 8th house from Lagna.

About 2½ years

Ardha Ashtama Sani

Saturn in the 4th from natal Moon

Traditionally associated with home, property, vehicles, domestic responsibilities and peace of mind. Other chart factors may substantially change an astrologer's interpretation.

Definition varies

Kantaka Sani

Often associated with the 7th; some traditions include the 4th and/or 10th from Moon

This is a regional/traditional label, not uniformly defined. A careful reference should state the exact house position and the tradition being followed.

A sign change, not a dosha

Sani Peyarchi

Saturn enters a new sidereal zodiac sign

Saturn changes signs roughly every 2½ years. The same ingress can begin or end a named Moon-based period, depending on the natal Moon sign.

3. How to analyse a Sani period

Step 1 — Confirm the reference point

For Ezharai Sani, Ashtama Sani and Ardha Ashtama Sani, count signs from the natal Moon. Separately inspect Saturn's transit house from Lagna. These are different calculations.

Step 2 — Check the natal Saturn

Traditional analysis considers Saturn's sign, house, dignity, lordship, aspects, conjunctions and strength. Functional benefic/malefic status depends on the ascendant and the school of interpretation.

Step 3 — Check Dasha and Bhukti

Read the running Mahadasha and Antardasha alongside the transit. Some astrologers give more weight to a transit when it connects with the houses or planets activated by the running Dasha.

Step 4 — Refine with transit details

Check retrograde and direct passes, aspects, conjunctions, nakshatra changes and sensitive natal degrees. A repeated pass is a repeated activation, not a new named Sani period.

Step 5 — Optional traditional refinements

Some astrologers use Bhinnashtakavarga, Sarvashtakavarga and Vedha rules to qualify a transit. Methods and weighting vary by tradition.

Keep predictions measured

A named transit alone cannot establish a specific outcome. Interpretations differ by chart and tradition; astrology's predictive claims are not scientifically established.

Key distinction
Ezharai Sani is 12th → 1st → 2nd from the natal Moon; Ashtama Sani is 8th from the Moon; Ardha Ashtama Sani is 4th from the Moon. A transit through the 8th from Lagna is a different calculation.
