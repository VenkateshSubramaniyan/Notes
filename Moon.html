Moon: Tithi or Rasi – which matters more?

Neither one alone. Tithi tells you how much light and strength the Moon carries. Rasi tells you how comfortable it is where it sits. A good reading needs both, then more.

A. The two independent roles

Tithi / Paksha

Depends on the Moon–Sun angle

  • Sets the Moon's Paksha Bala, its phase-based strength.
  • Drives its natural benefic or malefic classification in Parashari astrology.
  • Waxing Moon: generally benefic.
  • Waning Moon: generally malefic.
  • Near Amavasya: the Moon is at its weakest.
  • Near Purnima: maximum Paksha Bala.

Rasi / sign placement

Depends on where the Moon sits in the zodiac

  • Sets sign dignity and positional strength.
  • Taurus: exaltation.
  • Cancer: own sign.
  • Scorpio: debilitation.
  • Other signs: judge by the sign lord's relationship to the Moon (friend, neutral, enemy).
  • Shapes how effectively the Moon can express its significations: mind, emotion, mother, comfort.
Quick check on Paksha Bala. It is measured from the Moon–Sun angle: roughly angle ÷ 3 virupas (out of 60) up to 180°, then mirrored. So it is 0 at Amavasya and 60 at Purnima. That is why a young crescent has low Paksha Bala even though it is waxing.

B. Who decides benefic or malefic?

Short answer: the phase decides the Moon's natural nature. The sign does not.

  1. Natural classification comes from waxing or waning. An exalted Moon is not automatically benefic. A debilitated Moon is not automatically malefic.
  2. Sign gives dignity and positional strength. It answers "how well can this Moon perform?", not "is this Moon benefic?"
  3. Functional nature is a separate layer. It depends on your ascendant and which houses the Moon rules. A natural benefic can be functionally difficult for a given lagna.
  4. Everything else modifies the result. Aspects, conjunctions, combustion-related considerations (a Moon very close to the Sun is also a waning-phase issue), house placement and Neecha Bhanga all adjust the final reading.
A common simplification. Many texts do not call the Moon strongly benefic the instant it starts to wax. The widely used rule treats the Moon as strong and benefic from Shukla Ashtami to Krishna Saptami, and as weak and malefic-leaning from Krishna Ashtami to Shukla Saptami. Traditions differ on the exact cut-off, so the table and tool on this page follow this common rule and label young crescents as "weak".

The honest conclusion: you cannot rank these four boxes without the full chart. Shadbala adds five more strengths (Sthana, Dig, Kala, Cheshta, Naisargika) beyond Paksha Bala. Houses, aspects and conjunctions can lift or sink any box.

F. How the Moon behaves with other planets

The Moon is the mind, so the planet that joins it colours how the mind works. A strong Moon absorbs and uses the other planet's energy. A weak Moon is more impressionable and gets pulled around by it.

Planet with MoonStrong or benefic MoonWeak or malefic Moon

When the Moon is exalted or debilitated

The planet decides what kind of influence arrives. The Moon's sign decides how well the mind handles it. Tithi then decides how much strength the Moon has to back it up.

Exalted Moon (Taurus, peak near 3°)

  • The mind stays steady and comfortable, so it absorbs the other planet without being overwhelmed.
  • Good pairings get better. Venus, Mercury and Jupiter give refined, practical, wise results.
  • Hard pairings are softened but not erased. Saturn is still heavy and Rahu still hungry, but both are more manageable.
  • Watch the phase. An exalted Moon with the Sun is near Amavasya. The sign is dignified but the light is nearly gone.

Debilitated Moon (Scorpio, weakest near 3°)

  • The mind is intense, sensitive and easily swayed, so the other planet's effect goes deeper and feels more extreme.
  • Good pairings still help, but less smoothly. Jupiter steadies and protects.
  • Hard pairings hit harder. Saturn can bring worry and heaviness, Rahu fear and confusion.
  • Check Neecha Bhanga. It can turn this around (see below).

Neecha Bhanga checks for a Scorpio Moon

  • Mars is the dispositor, since Scorpio is Mars's sign. Mars in a kendra from the lagna or Moon helps. Mars with the Moon in Scorpio counts as a kendra from the Moon.
  • Venus is lord of Taurus, where the Moon is exalted. Venus in a kendra from the lagna or Moon helps.
  • Schools differ on how fully the cancellation applies, so always judge how strong the cancelling planet really is.

Aspects

Work like conjunctions, but gentler. Jupiter's aspect supports a weak Moon. Saturn's or Rahu's aspect adds pressure.

The Moon's effect on others

A weak Moon behaves like a mild malefic for the planets it joins. It can dilute their results, especially when very close to the Sun.

Sign and house still modify all of this. The same conjunction reads differently when the Moon is exalted, debilitated, or placed in a kendra or dusthana. Traditions differ on how strongly some yogas act, so use this as a starting point.
