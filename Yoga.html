Yogas of Jyotisha

A working reference to the planetary combinations classical Vedic astrology treats as auspicious (Shubha) and inauspicious (Ashubha) — with a South Indian style horoscope showing several of them in one chart.

Sign-based yoga rules are the classical (Parashari) layer. In KP, the same houses are re-examined through star-lord significators — a planet can carry a yoga's promise only if its nakshatra lord also signifies the relevant houses, which is why two charts with an identical sign-based yoga can still play out very differently.

Shubha Yogas — auspicious combinations

Formed mainly by kendra (1,4,7,10) and trikona (1,5,9) lords supporting one another, or by planets reaching own sign or exaltation in strong houses.

Raja Yoga

Kendra lord and trikona lord conjunct, in mutual aspect, or exchanging signs

The broadest category of "power yoga." It links a house of action (kendra) with a house of fortune (trikona), and is read as authority, rank, or a life that rises above its starting conditions — strength depends on how well-placed and unafflicted both lords are.

Dhana Yoga

Lords of 2, 5, 9, or 11 conjunct, exchanged, or mutually aspecting

The standard wealth-combination family. The 2nd (accumulated wealth), 11th (gains), 5th and 9th (fortune) lords reinforcing each other point to financial ease, though timing still runs through the dasha of the involved planets.

Gajakesari Yoga

Moon and Jupiter in mutual kendra (1st, 4th, 7th, or 10th from each other), including conjunction

One of the most cited yogas — traditionally read as intelligence, a good reputation, and steady respect from others. Its strength is graded by whether Moon and Jupiter are also well-disposed by sign and house.

Panch Mahapurusha Yogas

Mars, Mercury, Jupiter, Venus, or Saturn in its own sign or exaltation, posited in a kendra from the Lagna

Five yogas in one family — Ruchaka (Mars), Bhadra (Mercury), Hamsa (Jupiter), Malavya (Venus), and Sasa (Saturn) — each said to give a pronounced, almost archetypal strength in that planet's domain: drive, intellect, wisdom, refinement, or discipline respectively.

Budhaditya Yoga

Sun and Mercury conjunct in the same house

Read as sharp analytical ability and communication skill. Its result softens considerably if Mercury sits too close to the Sun and is combust rather than simply conjunct.

Chandra-Mangal Yoga

Moon and Mars conjunct, unafflicted by malefic aspect

Traditionally tied to business acumen and the ability to generate wealth through initiative and hard work, rather than inheritance.

Neechabhanga Raja Yoga

A debilitated planet's weakness is cancelled — e.g. its dispositor is in a kendra, or the sign lord of its exaltation sign is angular

A reversal yoga: a planet that looks weak by sign becomes a source of unusually strong, often delayed rise — commonly described as success built after early struggle.

Adhi Yoga (Chandra Adhi Yoga)

Natural benefics (Jupiter, Venus, unafflicted Mercury) occupying the 6th, 7th, and 8th houses from the Moon

Associated with leadership and lasting prosperity — the benefics effectively "guard" the houses on either side of the 7th from the Moon.

Vipreeta Raja Yoga

Lords of the 6th, 8th, and 12th houses conjoining or exchanging among themselves

A paradox yoga: houses that are ordinarily difficult (loss, disease, expenditure) end up strengthening each other instead of the native, producing gain that comes specifically out of overcoming adversity.

Amala Yoga

A benefic planet alone in the 10th house from the Lagna or from the Moon

Reputation that stays largely untarnished over a lifetime — "amala" means spotless, and the yoga is read as consistent public standing rather than a single dramatic rise.

Maha Dhana Yoga

Lords of the 2nd, 9th, and 11th houses connected by conjunction, mutual aspect, or exchange, and posited together

The most concentrated of the wealth combinations — treasury (2nd), fortune (9th), and gains (11th) all reinforcing one house or one another, traditionally read as substantial and lasting affluence rather than a single windfall.

Sunapha Yoga

Any planet other than the Sun placed in the 2nd house from the Moon

One of the five Chandra yogas — read as self-earned wealth, reputation, and intelligence, built through the native's own effort rather than inheritance.

Anapha Yoga

Any planet other than the Sun placed in the 12th house from the Moon

The counterpart to Sunapha — traditionally linked to good health, a well-formed physique, and a contented, comfortable temperament.

Durudhara Yoga

Planets other than the Sun occupying both the 2nd and 12th houses from the Moon

Combines Sunapha and Anapha in one chart — read as wealth accompanied by a strong circle of support, since "durudhara" evokes being held up on both sides.

Harsha · Sarala · Vimala Yogas

The 6th, 8th, or 12th lord respectively placed in another dusthana (6th, 8th, or 12th house)

Three named variants of the same Vipreeta Raja Yoga principle — Harsha (6th lord), Sarala (8th lord), and Vimala (12th lord) — each describing which dusthana lord lands in a dusthana, with the specific pairing said to color the flavor of the reversal.

Ashubha Yogas — inauspicious combinations

Formed mainly through dusthana (6,8,12) involvement, isolation of the Moon, or affliction from Rahu and Ketu.

Kemadruma Yoga

No planets (other than the Sun) in the 2nd or 12th house from the Moon, and the Moon unaspected in a kendra

Reads as an isolated Moon — instability of mind and a lack of steady support from circumstances or people, though the yoga is fully cancelled if any planet sits in a kendra from the Lagna or the Moon.

Kaal Sarp Yoga

All seven classical planets hemmed on one side of the Rahu–Ketu axis

Popularly the most talked-about dosha — read as recurring obstacles and a sense of fated delay, with outcomes said to depend heavily on which houses Rahu and Ketu themselves occupy.

Shakat Yoga

Moon in the 6th, 8th, or 12th house from Jupiter

Traditionally linked to sharp financial ups and downs across life — periods of comfort followed by unexpected reversal, rather than steady loss.

Guru Chandal Yoga

Jupiter conjunct Rahu

Jupiter's role as the significator of wisdom and ethics gets "clouded" by Rahu's amplifying, boundary-crossing nature — read as unconventional beliefs, or wisdom gained through unorthodox or difficult means rather than tradition.

Angarak Yoga

Mars conjunct Rahu

An intensifying combination — impulsiveness, accident-proneness, or conflict-seeking energy, especially in the house where it falls.

Grahan Yoga

Sun or Moon conjunct Rahu or Ketu

The luminary is "eclipsed" — vitality and confidence (Sun) or peace of mind (Moon) are said to fluctuate or feel obscured, particularly during that planet's dasha.

Daridra Yoga

The 11th lord placed in the 6th, 8th, or 12th house, or conjunct a dusthana lord

A drain on gains — income arrives but leaks out through debt, health costs, or hidden expenses rather than accumulating.

Vish Yoga

Moon conjunct Saturn

Read as an emotionally heavy, self-critical temperament — Saturn's restriction acting directly on the mind — though it can also indicate unusual discipline once matured.

Moon and Jupiter conjunct in the 4th house — a kendra from the Lagna — satisfying the mutual-kendra rule directly through conjunction.

Hamsa Mahapurusha Yoga

Jupiter is exalted in Cancer and sits in a kendra (4th) from the Lagna — the full condition for this Mahapurusha yoga.

Malavya + Sasa Mahapurusha Yoga

Venus (own sign, Libra) and Saturn (exalted in Libra) share the 7th house, a kendra — two Mahapurusha yogas overlapping in one house.

Kaal Sarp Yoga

Every other planet — Mars, Moon, Jupiter, Sun, Mercury, Venus, Saturn — falls within houses 3 through 7, entirely on one side of the Rahu (2nd house) to Ketu (12th house) axis, with nothing occupying the Lagna to break the hem.

Leo Lagna. Sun and Mercury conjunct in the 2nd house — Budhaditya Yoga. Separately, Saturn is debilitated in the 9th (Aries), but its dispositor Mars sits in the 10th (Taurus), a kendra — cancelling the debility (Neechabhanga) and turning it into a rise-after-struggle placement instead.

Harsha Yoga

Capricorn Lagna. Mercury, lord of the 6th house (Gemini), is placed in the 12th house (Sagittarius) — a dusthana lord landing in another dusthana. This is Harsha Yoga, the 6th-lord variant of Vipreeta Raja Yoga: a difficult house's lord finding strength precisely by sitting somewhere equally difficult.

Sagittarius Lagna. The Moon sits alone in the 9th (Leo) — the houses on either side of it (2nd and 12th from the Moon) are empty, and no planet occupies a kendra from either the Moon or the Lagna. Nothing "supports" the Moon, so the isolation reading applies in full.

Guru Chandal + Angarak Yoga

Gemini Lagna. Mars, Jupiter, and Rahu all sit together in the 8th house (Capricorn) — Mars-Rahu forms Angarak Yoga (impulsiveness, conflict) while Jupiter-Rahu forms Guru Chandal Yoga (clouded judgement) in the same breath, both intensified by falling in a dusthana.

These charts are teaching constructions, stacked with yogas for clarity — real horoscopes rarely show this many at once. Even here, each yoga's actual strength would still depend on shadbala, aspects from other planets, house lordship of the sign involved, and the timing of the relevant daśā before it could be called strong or weak.
